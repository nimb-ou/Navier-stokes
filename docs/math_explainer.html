1. The Millennium Prize Problem Context

In Charles Fefferman's official Clay Mathematics Institute formulation of the Navier–Stokes Existence and Smoothness Millennium Problem, four statements are posed:

  • (A) Existence and smoothness on $\mathbb{R}^3$ without force: Unforced smooth initial data gives global smooth solution with bounded energy.
  • (B) Existence and smoothness on $\mathbb{T}^3$ without force: Periodic unforced counterpart.
  • (C) Breakdown of Navier–Stokes on $\mathbb{R}^3$ with force: There exist smooth initial data $u_0$ and smooth compactly supported force $f$ for which no global smooth solution with uniformly bounded kinetic energy exists.
  • (D) Breakdown of Navier–Stokes on $\mathbb{T}^3$ with force: Periodic counterpart of breakdown.
The OpenAI Theorem: Proves alternatives (C) and (D)! For every viscosity $\nu > 0$, they explicitly construct a smooth, compactly supported force $f$ and a solution $u$ that starts from rest ($u(x,0) = 0$), keeps kinetic energy uniformly bounded $\sup_{t < 1} \|u(t)\|_{L^2} < \infty$, yet blows up at finite time $t = 1$: $$\limsup_{t \uparrow 1} \|u(t)\|_{L^\infty} = \infty$$

2. The Anisotropic Self-Similar Vortex Core

Singularity forms at the spatial origin $(0, 0, 0)$ at time $t = 1$. Let $\tau = 1 - t$ be the remaining time before the singularity.

The core contracts anisotropically (faster radially than axially):

$$ \ell_r \asymp \tau^{1/2}, \quad \ell_z \asymp \tau^{1/2 - h}, \quad \frac{\ell_r}{\ell_z} \asymp \tau^h \to 0 \quad (0 < h < 1/100) $$

Because $\ell_r / \ell_z \to 0$, the concentrating region becomes an infinitely thin, needle-like vortex column.

Velocity Scaling & Bounded Energy Paradox

The cylindrical velocity components scale as:

$$ |u_\theta^{(0)}|, |u_z^{(0)}| \asymp \tau^{-1/2 - h}, \quad |u_r^{(0)}| = O(\tau^{-1/2}) $$

Notice the miraculous volume vs. velocity balance:

  • Core Volume: $\mathrm{Vol}(C_\tau) \asymp \ell_r^2 \ell_z \asymp \tau \cdot \tau^{1/2 - h} = \tau^{3/2 - h}$.
  • Velocity Squared: $|u|^2 \asymp \tau^{-1 - 2h}$.
  • Core Kinetic Energy: $$E_{\rm core} \asymp \mathrm{Vol} \times |u|^2 \asymp \tau^{3/2 - h} \tau^{-1 - 2h} = \tau^{1/2 - 3h} \xrightarrow[\tau \to 0]{} 0$$

Since $h < 1/100 < 1/6$, $1/2 - 3h > 0$, so the kinetic energy in the concentrating core actually tends to zero despite velocities diverging to infinity!

3. The Physics: Inward Inflow, Spin-up, and Axial Ejection

Why doesn't fluid accumulate at the axis when $u_r < 0$? Incompressibility $\nabla \cdot u = 0$ requires: $$ \frac{1}{r}\partial_r(r u_r) + \partial_z u_z = 0 $$

Radial inflow carries angular momentum $r u_\theta$ inward. Viscosity diffuses it outward. The fluid spirals inward, but rather than stopping at $r=0$, the incoming mass is continuously evacuated by the axial outflow $u_z$, which points upwards for $z > 0$ and downwards for $z < 0$.

To break reflection symmetry (which would force $u_z(r, 0, t) = 0$ and kill axial shear at the dividing plane), the construction introduces a small upward offset $j_0 > 0$, separating the layer of weak rotation from the layer of vanishing axial shear.

4. Annular Wave Packets & Reynolds Stress Cancellation

If you join this self-similar core onto a smooth exterior heat flow, the momentum residual $\mathcal{R}(u, p) = \partial_t u + (u \cdot \nabla)u - \nu \Delta u + \nabla p$ is nonzero in the intervening annulus $X_a < X < X_b$ and diverges as $t \uparrow 1$.

To cancel this singular residual using smooth forcing, the authors inject high-frequency oscillatory wave packets $w$:

$$ \mathcal{R}(u_B + w, p_B + \pi) = \mathcal{R}(u_B, p_B) + \mathcal{L}_{u_B}(w, \pi) + \nabla \cdot (w \otimes w) $$

The nonlinear Reynolds stress $\langle w \otimes w \rangle$ has two wave families ($\sigma = \pm 1$) that extract kinetic energy from the background shear, grow, and then decay through viscous damping. Their averaged quadratic fluxes match and cancel the singular residual stress!
